# The Law of Root: Core System Rules & Appendices (October 2025)

## 1. Golden Rules
### 1.1 Rules Conflicts
* **1.1.1 Precedence:** If a card conflicts with the Law, follow the card. If the Learning to Play guide conflicts with the Law, follow the Law. If you can follow both a general rule and a faction or hireling rule, follow both; if you cannot, follow the faction or hireling rule.
* **1.1.2 Use of CANNOT:** The term CANNOT is absolute. It cannot be overridden unless explicitly instructed.
* **1.1.3 Unclear Resolutions and Choices:** Whenever it is unclear what order simultaneous effects should resolve in, or which player should make a decision, the player taking their turn chooses.

### 1.2 Public and Private Information
* **1.2.1 Hands:** Players may only show or reveal cards in their hand if explicitly instructed, but the number of cards in their hand is public information.
* **1.2.2 Piles and the Shared Deck:** Face-up piles such as the discard pile can be searched and inspected. The shared deck cannot.

### 1.3 Negotiation, Deals, and Consent
* **1.3.1 Agreements:** Players may discuss the game and make agreements, but they are non-binding.
* **1.3.2 Cards:** Players may only give cards to each other if explicitly instructed.
* **1.3.3 Consent:** Actions never require consent.

### 1.4 Game Structure
* **1.4.1 Turn Structure:** Each player's turn has three phases: Birdsong, Daylight, and Evening. Anything that says "at start of" a phase happens before everything else in the phase, and anything that says "at end of" happens after everything else in the phase but before the start of the next phase. After a player ends Evening, the next clockwise player begins their turn.
* **1.4.2 Interrupts:** You cannot interrupt an action, ability, or persistent effect with another effect unless it explicitly allows it.
* **1.4.3 End of Turn:** Once a player begins the last step of their turn, they cannot take actions except those that specify "end of Evening" or "end of turn".

### 1.5 Pieces
* **1.5.1 Limits:** Pieces are limited by the contents of the game. Do not use proxy pieces if you run out.
* **1.5.2 Starting Faction:** Generically, these are called FACTION PIECES. Specifically, these are called [FACTION NAME] PIECES.
* **1.5.3 Piece Ownership:** The ownership of faction pieces cannot change.
* **1.5.4 Piece Manipulation:** Pieces are placed and removed as defined in the Glossary. If prompted to place, take, or remove pieces but you cannot do so fully, you must place, take, or remove the maximum number possible. If multiple pieces are removed simultaneously and that would trigger effects, remove all pieces before triggering effects.
* **1.5.5 Use of FORCE:** Some effects let you FORCE a player or their pieces to act. Resolve this exactly as if that player were choosing to do this. When a player is forced to battle, they make all decisions within the battle itself.

## 2. Key Concepts
### 2.1 Cards
* **2.1.1 Birds Are Wild:** You can treat any bird card as a card of another suit.
* **2.1.1.I Forced Effects:** If prompted to discard or give cards of non-bird suits, you must treat bird cards as a prompted suit.
* **2.1.1.II Reverse Substitution:** If prompted to spend, discard, take, or give a bird card, you cannot substitute a card of another suit.
* **2.1.2 Ambush Cards:** There are five AMBUSH CARDS in the deck. They cannot be crafted. You may play one in battle to deal immediate hits.
* **2.1.3 Dominance Cards:** There are four DOMINANCE CARDS. They cannot be crafted. You may play one to permanently change your victory condition.
* **2.1.4 Your Play Area:** When you activate a dominance card, craft persistent effects, or reveal cards, place them in your PLAY AREA. They are not in your hand.

### 2.2 Clearings and Paths
* **2.2.1 Adjacency:** A clearing is adjacent to all other clearings linked to it by a path.
* **2.2.2 Suit:** Each clearing has a suit: mouse, rabbit, or fox.
* **2.2.3 Slots:** Each clearing has a number of BUILDING SLOTS. Whenever you place a building, it fills an open slot.
* **2.2.4 Ruins:** Slots marked with "R" begin the game filled with RUINS. Ruins cannot be removed unless explicitly instructed.

### 2.3 Rivers & 2.4 Forests
* **2.3.1 Rivers:** Rivers are not paths, but can be treated as paths if explicitly instructed.
* **2.4.1 Forests:** Areas enclosed by paths and clearings are FORESTS. A forest is adjacent to all clearings that touch it, and adjacent to all forests separated by only one printed path.

### 2.5 Rule
* **2.5.1 Definition:** A player RULES a clearing if they have more total warriors and buildings in it than each other player. Tokens and pawns do not contribute to rule. If there is a tie, no one rules it.

## 3. Victory & Scoring
### 3.1 How to Win
* **3.1.1 Points:** The first player to reach 30 victory points immediately wins.
* **3.1.2 Ties:** If multiple players reach 30 points simultaneously, the tied player closest in clockwise order to the current player wins.

### 3.2 Scoring Victory Points
* **3.2.1 Removing Pieces:** Whenever you remove an enemy's building or token, score one victory point.
* **3.2.2 Crafting Items:** Whenever you craft an item, score the victory points listed on the card.

### 3.3 Dominance Cards Activation
* **3.3.1 Activating:** During Daylight, if you have $\ge 10$ points, you may ACTIVATE a dominance card from your hand. Remove your score marker; you can no longer score victory points.
    * **I. Suit Dominance:** Win immediately if you rule 3 clearings of the matching suit at the start of your Birdsong.
    * **II. Bird Dominance:** Win immediately if you rule 2 clearings in opposite corners at the start of your Birdsong.
* **3.3.3 Using Dominance Cards:** Anytime a dominance card would be placed in the discard pile, instead place it near the map to show it is AVAILABLE.
* **3.3.4 Taking Available Cards:** During Daylight, you can take an available dominance card into your hand by spending a matching card. You cannot treat an available bird dominance card as a non-bird suit.

## 4. Key Actions
### 4.1 Craft
* **4.1.1 Cost:** To craft, you must activate crafting pieces of the suits listed on the card. A crafting piece's suit matches its clearing. Each piece may be activated once per turn. A three-color question mark means a crafting piece of any suit.
* **4.1.2 Immediate Effects:** Resolve its effect and discard the card. If it shows an item, take it from the supply. If the item is not in the supply, the card cannot be crafted.
* **4.1.3 Persistent Effects:** Place it in your play area to gain the described effect. 
* **4.1.4 No Duplicates:** You cannot craft a persistent effect if you have an identical one in your play area.

### 4.2 Move
* **4.2.1 Rule Needed:** Take any number of your warriors/pawns from one clearing and move them on a path to an adjacent clearing. You must rule the origin, destination, or both.

### 4.3 Battle Steps
* **4.3.1 Step 1: Defender May Ambush:** Defender may play one matching ambush card.
    * **I. Foil Ambush:** Attacker may play a matching ambush to cancel it.
    * **II. Resolve Ambush:** Defender deals 2 hits immediately. If no attackers remain, end battle.
* **4.3.2 Step 2: Use Before-Roll Effects:** Players use "before rolling" effects.
* **4.3.3 Step 3: Roll Dice:** Roll both dice. Attacker takes higher roll; defender takes lower roll.
    * **I. Maximum Rolled Hits:** Maximum rolled hits equals the number of your warriors in the clearing of battle.
* **4.3.4 Step 4: Use After-Roll Effects:** Use effects that affect battle but do not specify before rolling.
* **4.3.5 Step 5: Count Hits:** Sum rolled hits and effects.
    * **I. Extra Hits:** Extra hits are not limited by the number of warriors.
    * **II. Defenseless:** If defender has no warriors, attacker deals an extra hit.
    * **III. Ignored Hits:** Ignored hits subtract from total.
* **4.3.6 Step 6: Deal Hits:** Each hit removes one piece. Side taking hits chooses order, but all warriors must be removed before buildings or tokens.

## 5. Setup
### 5.1 Standard Setup
* **5.1.1 Step 1:** Assign factions and starting player. 
* **5.1.2 Step 2:** Place score markers on "0".
* **5.1.3 Step 3:** Shuffle deck, draw 3 cards. (Remove dominance cards in a 2-player game).
* **5.1.4 Step 4:** Place 4 ruins total in slots marked with "R".
* **5.1.5 Step 5:** Form item supply.

## Appendix G. Glossary
* **G.1 Ability:** A persistent effect of a faction or hireling.
* **G.4 Cannot:** Absolute restriction, see rule 1.1.2.
* **G.8 Enemy:** Any other player not in a coalition with you.
* **G.9 Enemy Piece:** Faction or hireling piece of an enemy player. Items are not enemy pieces.
* **G.20 Place:** Put a piece into a destination from its supply.
* **G.23 Replace:** Remove a piece and place another in its location. Must be able to complete both steps to execute.
* **G.28 Rule:** Rule is established by having the most warriors and buildings combined.
* **G.36 Warrior:** A wooden figure owned by its faction. The Vagabond pawn is not a warrior and cannot be removed.

## Appendix L. Landmarks
* **L.1.1 Safety:** Landmarks cannot be battled, moved, covered, or removed unless noted.
* **L.1.2 Ownership:** Landmarks are not owned by anyone and are not enemy pieces.

## Appendix M. Maps
* **M.1 Autumn Map:** No special rules.
* **M.2 Winter Map:** The raging river divides forests like paths do.
* **M.3 Lake Map:** Clearings touching the lake are Coastal Clearings. The Ferry allows moving to another coastal clearing once per turn, ignoring paths. Moving player draws a card.
* **M.4 Mountain Map:** Contains Closed Paths. Clearings linked by a closed path are not adjacent. A player can spend a card during Daylight to remove a closed path marker permanently and score 1 point. Control of The Pass at the end of Evening scores 1 victory point.
* **M.5 Marsh Map:** Flooded clearings are no longer clearings. They only contain paths or flooded paths. Clearings linked by non-flooded paths are adjacent.
